# What UVM assets and findings are, what the Assets and Findings pages show, and how on-demand asset scans work (uvm.assets-findings)
Also called: uvm assets; uvm findings; crown jewel asset; on-demand asset scan; finding evidence

**Assets.** A UVM asset represents a single asset in the environment, unified (merged) and enriched with information from multiple sources; the Assets page lets users see the sources and records it was merged from, its findings and its related tickets. [uvm.assets-findings:asset-definition] On the Assets page, Risk Score reflects the highest severity score among the asset's findings and is initially set by the default reconciliation function, which can be customized through Data Unification; Risk Mass is the sum of severity scores of the asset's active findings. [uvm.assets-findings:asset-risk-score] Is Crown Jewel is a boolean (TRUE or FALSE) field indicating whether the asset is defined as one of the organization's most valuable assets, and Assets can be filtered by Asset Is Crown Jewel, State, Owner ID or Source. [uvm.assets-findings:asset-crown-jewel] The Assets overview charts show assets by risk score in increments of 0.5 (X-axis the max severity score of active findings) and the 5 most frequent asset types and operating systems. [uvm.assets-findings:asset-charts] With the default Active saved view selected, the details shown for each asset include Total Findings (the sum of active findings per severity), First Seen and Last Seen (earliest and latest finding detection dates on the asset), and Tags pulled from sources. [uvm.assets-findings:asset-columns] EASM-discovered assets are shown by selecting EASM Assets in the Sources filter, and the Available Asset Types listed are Certificate (SSL/TLS certificates), Domain (domains, subdomains and hosts), Edge Server (IP addresses) and Git Repository (GitHub repositories). [uvm.assets-findings:easm-asset-types]

**Findings.** A UVM finding is a vulnerability or misconfiguration detected on an asset and contextualized from multiple sources (such as CVE details, exposed secrets or misconfigurations); findings are grouped into tickets. [uvm.assets-findings:finding-definition] The Findings overview compares original severity scores with recalculated contextualized scores per severity category, shows the top 5 sources by finding count, and a Finding Duplication chart of findings retrieved from different sources. [uvm.assets-findings:findings-charts] Create Ticket on the Findings page splits the selected findings from their current ticket into a new ticket that is locked, with the originating tickets listed on its Related Tickets tab. [uvm.assets-findings:findings-create-ticket] The Viewing Findings in UVM article lists Available Finding Types: CVE (vulnerabilities, commonly exploited CVEs and vulnerabilities supported by Threat Intelligence decoys in Zscaler Deception) and Misconfig (misconfigurations and exposures). [uvm.assets-findings:finding-types] The Misconfig finding type includes exposed VPN appliances, exposed sensitive services, revealing hostnames, non-default or unknown open ports, expired domain registrations, domains with registration expiring within 30 days, expired SSL/TLS certificates, outdated SSL/TLS versions, self-signed certificates, SSL/TLS certificates expiring within 30 days, HTTP using insecure header, HTTP missing common security header and dangling DNS records. [uvm.assets-findings:misconfig-list] The finding drawer's Finding Evidence tab information is only available for findings ingested using Zscaler's EASM capabilities, and shows the Scan Output (labeled by scan type, such as HTTP Response, Nmap Response and Certificate Response) and Match Evidence, the portions of the scan results that attest to the finding. [uvm.assets-findings:finding-evidence] EASM-discovered findings are shown by selecting EASM Findings in the Sources filter. [uvm.assets-findings:easm-findings-filter]

**On-demand asset scans.** On-demand asset scans from UVM are in limited availability (contact Zscaler Support) and need assets ingested by a configured data source connector, an active scanner outegration, and Read and Edit permissions under the Vulnerabilities App - Asset Operational View resource. [uvm.assets-findings:scan-feature-prereq] The SecOps platform currently supports the Tenable Vulnerability Management and Tenable Security Center scanner outegrations for triggering scans, and scans can be triggered only on assets ingested from a source that has a corresponding outegration. [uvm.assets-findings:scan-scanners] A maximum of 10 assets can be selected per scan. [uvm.assets-findings:scan-max-assets] The number of concurrent scans is set by the Max Number of Parallel Scans setting in the scanner outegration; scans cannot be stopped in UVM and must be cancelled in the scanner interface. [uvm.assets-findings:scan-limits] The scanner queues and executes the scan job according to its own scheduling and capacity; the outegration automatically polls for final results and updates the asset data, with a possible short delay while queued ETL operations are processed; progress and completion can be monitored in the scanner outegration's activity log. [uvm.assets-findings:scan-flow]
