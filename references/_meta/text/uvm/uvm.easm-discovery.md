# How EASM discovery profiles work: seeds and their formats, limits, scan cadence, permissions and reports (uvm.easm-discovery)
Also called: discovery profile; easm seeds; external attack surface management discovery; discovery settings

EASM discovery uses methods and data sources including WHOIS reverse lookup, ICANN regex analysis, DNS record lookup, web content analysis, certificate transparency logs, GitHub repository scanning, Nuclei patterns, certificate analysis and LLM subnet and IP inferencing. [uvm.easm-discovery:discovery-methods] Discovery starts from one or more seed assets and expands through successive layers of connected assets using passive and active scanning; ownership is validated automatically by a proprietary LLM comparing WHOIS fields such as Registrant Organization and Registrant Email against seed domains, and a discovery chain is presented; scans run weekly. [uvm.easm-discovery:discovery-process]

**Risk detection.** EASM risk assessment identifies exposures such as CVEs, misconfigurations, open ports, expired certificates or domains, phishing and lookalike domains, exposed database services and outdated TLS versions, with an out-of-the-box risk score that is combined with other SecOps platform risk context. [uvm.easm-discovery:risk-detections] Outdated or irrelevant discovered assets are marked inactive depending on the asset aging configuration, and results appear in AEM (asset inventory) and UVM (risks and findings). [uvm.easm-discovery:inactive-aging]

**Access.** On the SecOps platform, access to EASM capabilities requires AEM or UVM or both, and configuring and managing discovery settings requires Read, Create, Edit and Delete permissions for the EASM Configuration resource. [uvm.easm-discovery:access] Managing (editing and deleting) discovery profiles requires Read, Edit and Delete permissions for the EASM Configuration resource. [uvm.easm-discovery:edit-delete-permission] Exporting a discovery profile report requires Read permission under the EASM Configuration resource, and the report is generated and downloaded as a PPTX file. [uvm.easm-discovery:export-permission]

**Seeds.** A seed is a known legitimate asset such as a domain, IP address or IP address block, and an organization name can also be used as a seed for reverse domain lookup. [uvm.easm-discovery:seed-types] A discovery profile can have a maximum of 100 seeds across the seed types. [uvm.easm-discovery:seed-limit] Excluding domains, IP addresses, IP blocks or organizations requires first including at least one seed of any type. [uvm.easm-discovery:exclude-needs-include] A discovery profile groups relevant seeds by including or excluding seeds across asset types, and separate profiles can be created for different business entities such as a parent company and its subsidiaries. [uvm.easm-discovery:profile-per-entity] [divergence:uvm.easm-discovery:profiles-per-entity] [The discovery profiles page says that currently only one discovery profile can be configured for an organization and that its Add New Discovery button disappears once a profile is added; the creation steps call the button Add Discovery Profile, and the organization page speaks of distinct discovery profiles for each custom organization.](kb:easm.discovery:profile-one-per-org)

**Seed formats.** IP seed addresses must be strictly formatted IPv4; subnet mask, port number and CIDR notation are not supported, and IPv6 is not supported. [uvm.easm-discovery:ip-format] IP block seeds must be an IPv4 address followed by a slash and an unsigned integer prefix between 20 and 32 inclusive; prefixes from 0 to 19 and explicit signs are not allowed. [uvm.easm-discovery:block-format] Domain seeds support domains, subdomains, top-level domains and punycode extensions, and organization seed names must be at least 4 characters. [uvm.easm-discovery:domain-org-format] Domain, IP address and IP block seeds can be uploaded from a CSV file with each entry in a separate row and no header row required. [uvm.easm-discovery:csv-format]

**Scanning and profile changes.** Discovery profile scan frequency is set automatically to Weekly and cannot be changed; modifying the seeds in a profile triggers a new scan, and a scan typically takes a few hours. [uvm.easm-discovery:scan-weekly] The EASM Settings page lists each discovery profile with its name, counts of included and excluded seeds, last scan timestamp and scan status; the Status column tracks progress, successful completion and failure. [uvm.easm-discovery:profile-list] After a scan completes, the ingested assets are viewed on the Assets page (the page places the unified asset inventory in AEM) and the associated risk detections on the Findings page. [uvm.easm-discovery:results-location] Existing seeds in a discovery profile cannot be edited, only removed and re-added; adding or removing seeds triggers a new scan. [uvm.easm-discovery:edit-profile] Deleting a discovery profile removes all references to its scan data, including discovered assets and associated risk findings. [uvm.easm-discovery:delete-profile] Limits on the number of profiles and the aging default are open [gap:uvm.easm-discovery:profile-limits]; aging rules are in [Asset aging rules in the Agentic SecOps Platform: inactive assets, aging by time or value, affected entities and rule order](kb:unified.secops-platform.data.asset-aging).

**Reports.** Discovery profile reports include metrics such as known vulnerabilities, exposed servers and public cloud instances, outdated SSL/TLS, domain expiration and registered lookalike domains, and the report data varies by discovery profile. [uvm.easm-discovery:report-contents] The exported discovery profile reports are described as usable for executive- and board-level communication, for summarizing the inherited risks of a target company in mergers and acquisitions, and for auditing to demonstrate continuous monitoring. [uvm.easm-discovery:report-purposes]
