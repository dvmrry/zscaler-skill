# The AI Guard PII detector: categories, what it catches and misses, masking, entities and threshold (ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii)
Also called: personally identifiable information detector; pii detector runbook; ai guard pii threshold; ai guard allow masking

The Personally Identifiable Information (PII) detector flags user prompts that contain real personal information (the runbook lists credit card numbers, SSNs, emails, phone numbers, government IDs and bank identifiers) and ignores look-alike content that carries no real identifying data, such as test card numbers or fake SSNs. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:what-it-flags] This subject is the PII detector runbook; the detector list is in [AI Guard's detectors: the capabilities Help lists and the detectors the policy editor configures](kb:ai-security.ai-guard.detectors) and the settings every detector shares are in [AI Guard policies: detector settings, policy control rules, first-match order, applications and testing](kb:ai-security.ai-guard.policies).

**Categories.** The runbook's table lists these PII categories, each with an example value: Credit Card, Email, Phone, IP Address, IBAN, SWIFT Code, Date of Birth, US SSN, US ITIN, US Passport and US Bank Number. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:categories]

**What it catches.** The runbook says the detector catches valid PII in plain prose, JSON, XML, SQL, log lines and code, with or without surrounding context, each of several values in one prompt independently, a value that is not a perfect format match (typo, missing digit, slight formatting issue) when the surrounding context clearly identifies it as PII, and masked card references such as ---1234 or card ending in 5904, which are flagged intentionally. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:what-it-catches]

**What it misses.** The runbook's list of reasons PII does not trigger includes invalid SSNs (area 000, area 900+) and similar government-ID values, which are rejected on purpose even with strong context; heavy obfuscation (base64, hex, reversed digits, zero-width characters); PII split across labels; unusual separators (pipe, comma, slash, parenthesis), where separator handling beyond - and space is in active development; and too many individual PII categories disabled in the policy. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:misses]

**Document position.** A result containing start: null, end: null, which can appear when AI is used to analyze a document, means the PII detector found PII but could not determine its position in the document. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:start-null]

**Settings.** The PII tile adds Allow Masking, which when enabled masks the PII information when shown in the dashboard, and Entities, the list of PII labels to watch for, each with its own Action override (Detect, Allow, Block or Disabled); the runbook advises starting with three to five high-value PII labels. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:settings] The PII threshold defaults to 0.5, chosen on Zscaler's internal validation set of 28 languages and roughly 9,000 labeled prompts, and Zscaler strongly recommends leaving it at 0.5; the runbook's ranges are 0.7 to 1.00 (stricter, only high-confidence matches trigger, for sensitivity to noise from the extended PERSON, LOCATION and DATE/TIME categories), 0.5 to 0.69 (the default band, for production deployments) and 0.3 to 0.49 (more permissive, best when extended categories are restricted via entity). [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:threshold] The detector's own language support is open [gap:ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:language-support].

**Unexpected triggers.** The runbook points to Dashboard > Transaction Details > Detection Summary for which specific PII categories were detected, so the policy can be updated accordingly. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.pii:false-positive-check]
