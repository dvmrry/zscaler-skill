# The AI Guard Competition detector: the evidence rule, competitor list, tickers, languages, threshold and the scoreByCompetitor log field (ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.competition)
Also called: competitor detector; ai guard competition detector; scoreByCompetitor

The Competition detector flags user prompts that reference monitored competitors, with built-in tolerance for name misspellings, ticker symbol usage and multilingual input; the user provides a list of competitor names, and the runbook lists plain mentions, typos and OCR artifacts (with business context), other languages, ticker symbols and phonetic spellings as cases it triggers on. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.competition:what-it-flags] This subject is the Competition detector runbook; the detector list is in [AI Guard's detectors: the capabilities Help lists and the detectors the policy editor configures](kb:ai-security.ai-guard.detectors).

**The evidence rule.** The runbook says every potential match must have evidence that it refers to a company, not just a common word, and no evidence means no trigger regardless of length or spelling; its examples that do not trigger are a city block, Peter Pan, writer's block, Times Square and out of stock, and a lowercase mention with no business words, such as I like oracle, does not trigger, which the runbook calls intentional. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.competition:evidence-rule]

**Competitor list.** The Competitors field is a list of up to 10 names to watch for, each with its own Action override (Allow, Block or Detect); the runbook advises starting with three to five high-value competitors, using the common short form of a name, noting the detector already knows corporate suffixes (Inc, Corp, Ltd), and the full name for multi-word names, and says more than 10 per policy increases noise and latency, in which case the user probably wants the Topic detector instead. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.competition:competitor-list] Each policy has its own Competition configuration, and the runbook says to copy-paste the competitor list when duplicating a policy. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.competition:per-policy]

**Tickers and languages.** The runbook says the detector ships with a built-in ticker map covering the largest tech and finance tickers and tries fuzzy matching for others, with less reliable matches; it answers yes that 28 languages are supported out of the box, says capitalization evidence is English-only (in other languages the detector relies on business context alone), and says prompts under about 10 characters do not give the model enough context to classify reliably. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.competition:languages-tickers] The 28-language count is disputed: [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors:supported-languages](kb:ai-security.ai-guard.detectors:supported-languages).

**Threshold.** The Competition threshold defaults to 0.7, which Zscaler strongly recommends keeping; the runbook's ranges are 0.85 to 1.00 (only unambiguous mentions trigger), 0.70 to 0.84 (the default band, for production deployments), 0.50 to 0.69 (also fires on semantically adjacent cases, where the competitor is mentioned only indirectly or with ambiguous wording) and below 0.50 (very permissive, loose or tangential mentions trigger); it calls scores of 0.70 to 0.75 borderline, with both true and false positives expected, and says raising the threshold to 0.80 cuts most of these but also drops real matches. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.competition:threshold]

**Logs and support.** The Competition detector's triggers are tagged with a per-competitor scoreByCompetitor dict, so individual competitors can be pivoted on in a BI tool or spreadsheet; the runbook's support-ticket list for persistent false positives includes the confidence score from scoreByCompetitor and whether the prompt was in English or another language. [ai-security.ai-guard.detectors.competition:score-field]
