# The AI Security family: AI Access Security, AI Guard (AI Guardrails), AI Red Teaming and the AI asset inventory (ai-security.family)
Also called: ai security; zscaler ai security; ai security overview; ai guardrails; ai access security; ai asset management; ai red teaming; red teaming api; airt; which zscaler product secures ai; genai security; ai governance; ai guard vs ai guardrails; difference between ai guard and ai guardrails; ai security pillars; secure access to ai apps; ai security in the zscaler stack; sensitive data going into llm prompts; ai guard pricing; ai security licensing

Zscaler groups its AI products into one family. This subject says which part of the family answers which question before an answer goes deep.

**The four capabilities.** Zscaler's AI Security product page names four areas: AI Asset Management (auto-discovering AI assets, including models, agents, MCP servers and shadow AI, and tracing data-to-AI lineage across 2,900+ apps); Secure Access to AI (blocking access by role in real time, filtering sensitive data from LLMs, governing agent-to-agent traffic); Secure AI Apps and Infrastructure (testing 5,000+ attack scenarios across text, image, voice and documents, mapped to MITRE ATLAS, NIST and the EU AI Act); and AI Gateway (routing, securing and governing AI transactions from one control plane, with inline DLP and Zero Trust applied to every model, agent and API call). [ai-security.family:four-capabilities] The same page's FAQ says Zscaler AI Security protects the full AI life cycle by combining automated red teaming, dynamic risk assessment and advanced guardrails, and describes AI red teaming as testing and securing AI systems, especially LLMs, by simulating real-world attacks and vulnerabilities such as prompt injection or data poisoning. [ai-security.family:sub-products]

**AI Access Security.** Zscaler's AI Access Security page, titled Secure Access to AI, says it controls access and content across popular GenAI apps, AI SaaS, embedded AI and dev tools, and lists: detecting and classifying thousands of AI apps, including AI embedded in SaaS, with visibility into users, departments, application trends and at-risk data; prompt and response extraction and classification; allowing, blocking or coaching access by user or user group, and policies to warn, block or enforce browser isolation with control over copy-paste; inline DLP on prompts across 100+ DLP dictionaries such as Source Code, PII, PCI and PHI; detecting off-topic or policy-violating use, including toxic, restricted or competitive topics, in prompts and responses; and inline controls for AI IDEs and tools connecting to AI infrastructure. [ai-security.family:access-security-capabilities] The policy mechanics behind it are ZIA's: AI and ML rules in Cloud App Control, [AI & ML Applications rules: granular actions, prompt capture, tenant profiles](kb:zia.cloud-app-control.ai-ml-rules), prompt DLP in [ZIA Data Loss Prevention](kb:zia.dlp), AI URL categories in [ZIA URL Filtering](kb:zia.url-filtering), decryption in [ZIA SSL/TLS inspection](kb:zia.ssl-inspection), and isolation in [What sends traffic to Cloud Browser: Internet & SaaS rules, Smart Isolation and the Private Access isolation policy](kb:zbi.policy).

**AI Guard and AI Guardrails.** Zscaler's AI Guardrails product address now serves the AI Guard for Users page, which describes AI access decided per user, per app and per risk across 3500+ AI tools with four answers (allow, block, isolate or caution), and names AI Guard for Apps, which inspects prompts and responses in real time inside approved tools, as part of Zscaler AI Security. [ai-security.family:guardrails-page-is-ai-guard] The guardrail list an earlier capture of that page gave is unverified [gap:ai-security.family:guardrails-page-list]. AI Guardrails is a product-page name for AI Guard, not a separate configurable product: no Help tree or API carries the AI Guardrails name. [ai-security.family:guardrails-no-separate-surface] AI Guard is in [AI Guard: runtime guardrails on prompts and responses, its Proxy and DAS/API modes, and where each part is documented](kb:ai-security.ai-guard).

**APIs.** The captured AI Security API specification combines two families: AI Infrastructure, 17 operations served from `https://api.zsapi.net/aisecurity/aispm`, and AI Red Teaming, 97 operations served from `https://api.zsapi.net/aisecurity/airt`. [ai-security.family:api-families] The OneAPI base paths are in [The guide's base paths are: ZIA `/zia/api/v1`; ZPA `/zpa/mgmtconfig/v1`, `/zpa/mgmtconfig/v2` and `/zpa/userconfig/v1` (the last for SCIM attribute and group endpoints); ZDX `/zdx/v1`; Authentication Service `/ziam/admin/api/v1`; Client Connector `/zcc/papi/public/v1`; Cloud & Branch Connector `/ztw/api/v1`; Business Insights `/bi/api/v1`; EASM `/easm/easm-ui/v1`; Cellular `/zcell/config/api/v1`; Event Monitoring & Notification `/event-monitoring`; Unified RBAC `/urbac/urbac`; AI Security `/aisecurity/aispm/v1` (AI Infrastructure) and `/aisecurity/airt/api/v2` (AI Red Teaming); AI Guard `/aiguard/v1`.](kb:shared.oneapi.base-urls:base-path-table). The AI Infrastructure inventory and its paging rules are in [The AI Security inventory API (AI Infrastructure, AI-SPM): read-only assets and issues, cursor paging, filters and limits](kb:ai-security.asset-inventory-api); AI Guard has its own API, in [The AI Guard configuration API: behavior the contract states, limits and errors, and what the Python SDK covers](kb:ai-security.ai-guard.admin-api) and [Calling AI Guard at runtime: the proxy endpoint, the DAS detection API, its host, direction and policy ID, and throttling in the Python SDK](kb:ai-security.ai-guard.runtime-api).

**AI Red Teaming.** AI Red Teaming is the testing side of the family and AI Guard the runtime side: the product page's FAQ describes AI red teaming as testing AI systems by simulating real-world attacks such as prompt injection, and its 97 API operations (AI apps and integration tests, probes, test runs, scheduled tests, reports, remediation) are generated in the aisec surface. [ai-security.family:red-teaming-api] The console, connections, probes, test runs and brokers are in [AI Red Teaming: adversarial testing of AI apps, the Overview page, and where each part is documented](kb:ai-security.ai-red-teaming). What the captures do not settle about it is open [gap:ai-security.family:red-teaming-boundary].
