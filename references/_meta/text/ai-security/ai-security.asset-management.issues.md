# How AI Asset Management issues are generated, rated, moved between statuses and resolved or snoozed (ai-security.asset-management.issues)
Also called: ai asset management issues; issue status; snooze issue; resolve issue; model scan findings

An issue is the outcome of a policy evaluation done on a resource type; in the vendor's example, 5 publicly exposed S3 buckets containing sensitive data under an enabled detection policy give 5 issues. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:issue-definition] The Issues View page (AI Infrastructure > Issues > Issues View) displays issues with an open status by default. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:issues-default-view] An issue's risk level is calculated as the average of the policy severity and the resource impact; a Critical policy severity with Medium resource impact gives High. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:issue-risk-level] Issue Age is the number of days an issue was in an open state; it starts from zero when an issue is generated or reset (generated November 1st is 0, and November 5th is 4). [ai-security.asset-management.issues:issue-age] An issue's page has Issue Details and Remediation tabs; Issue Details shows the MITRE ATT&CK technique and ID, policy severity, policy category, compliance benchmark and compliance category, and Remediation shows the steps to resolve the issue in the CSP. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:issue-details-fields]

**Status.** The Issues page lists the issue status values Open, Closed, Resolved and Snoozed; issues are first generated as Open. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:issue-status-values] An automatically resolved issue is reopened if the violation is detected within 180 days of resolving it. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:open-reopen-180] When a data store is removed from the scan settings, its issues remain open based on the posture assessment of the data store. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:open-data-store-removed] Closed is the final state of an issue and does not move to any other state; a closed issue is deleted from the AI Security Admin Portal after 180 days. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:closed-final] The listed example scenarios for a Closed issue are: the cloud account is offboarded, the cloud account is deleted from the CSP, the policy is edited, disabled or deleted, the data store is deleted, and a removed data store is not added back to the scan settings. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:closed-causes] For closed and resolved issues the issue graph is frozen and its nodes are unclickable, and the Sensitive Data tab on the issue's page is hidden. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:frozen-graph] A resolved issue moves to Closed if the data store, policy or cloud account is deleted; a manually resolved issue stays resolved until reset, and a reset updates the status from the latest policy findings. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:resolved-causes]

**Resolve and snooze.** Resolving an issue requires an administrator role or a custom role with the Resolve Issues permission, which also grants permission to reset manually resolved issues. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:resolve-permission] A manually resolved issue is resolved only in the AI Security Admin Portal; the policy violation persists in the cloud service provider, and the issue stays resolved until reset. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:resolve-local-only] Snoozing an issue requires an administrator role or a custom role with the Snooze Issues permission, which also grants permission to reset snoozed issues; by default an issue is snoozed for 1 day. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:snooze-permission-default] Snoozing pauses issue notifications, which are not shared with recipients or third-party integrations within the snooze period; unsnoozing updates the issue status from the latest policy findings. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:snooze-notifications] Per the Managing Issues page, a snoozed issue remains snoozed for the specified duration if the issue is resolved in the CSP or if the resource or policy is deleted. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:snoozed-survives-delete] Per the Issue Status page, if the data store or policy is deleted while an issue is snoozed, the issue moves to Closed irrespective of the snooze period; at snooze expiry or unsnooze the status becomes open or resolved from the latest policy findings. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:snoozed-delete-closes]

**Model scan findings.** Model Scan Findings (AI Infrastructure > Issues > Model Scan Findings) groups findings by Model ID with a finding count or lists All Findings per scan, and a scan instance can be archived from the All Findings list. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:scan-findings-views] On a Model Scan Findings scan's General Information, the AI model risk score ranges are Low 0–25, Medium 26–50, High 51–75 and Critical 76–100. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:scan-findings-risk-bands] Each vulnerability in a model scan has a name, an ID identifying the detection logic (example Z-AISPM-SEC-014), category, artifact type and remediation; the examples given are GCP Service Account Private Key, code_execution and raw_text. [ai-security.asset-management.issues:scan-findings-vulnerability]

What happens to a snoozed issue when its resource or policy is deleted is in dispute [divergence:ai-security.asset-management.issues:snoozed-delete], and so is what disabling a policy does to existing issues [divergence:ai-security.asset-management.issues:policy-disabled-effect]. The policies that raise issues are in [How AI Posture Policies, threat categories and DLP data classification work in AI Asset Management](kb:ai-security.asset-management.posture-policies), the assets they apply to are in [What the AI Asset Management console inventories show: public AI apps, AI assets, AI resources, dashboard and usage insights](kb:ai-security.asset-management.discovery), and the hub is [AI Asset Management in the AI Security Admin Portal: what it inventories, signing in, first-time configuration order, table behavior](kb:ai-security.asset-management).

What to check:
1. Whether the issue was snoozed when its data store or policy was deleted: the Managing Issues page says it stays snoozed, the Issue Status page says it moves to Closed.
2. Whether a policy was disabled or edited: Issue Status lists these among the Closed scenarios, while Managing Policies says disabling leaves existing issues unchanged.

Sources disagree (name both sides):
- Managing Issues says a snoozed issue stays snoozed when its resource or policy is deleted, while Issue Status says it moves to Closed irrespective of the snooze period. [divergence:ai-security.asset-management.issues:snoozed-delete]
  - zscaler-help: A snoozed issue remains snoozed for the specified duration if the resource or policy is deleted.
  - zscaler-help: If the data store or policy is deleted while the issue is snoozed, the issue moves to Closed irrespective of the snooze period.
  Impact: The captured pages do not settle which status a snoozed issue holds after its policy is deleted; the status shown in the console decides.
- Issue Status lists a disabled policy among the scenarios that close an issue, while Managing Policies says the status of existing issues remains unchanged when a policy is disabled. [divergence:ai-security.asset-management.issues:policy-disabled-effect]
  - zscaler-help: A policy being edited, disabled or deleted is a listed scenario for a Closed issue.
  - zscaler-help: New issues are not generated for disabled policies, and the status of existing issues remains unchanged.
  Impact: The pages do not agree on whether disabling a policy closes its existing issues.
