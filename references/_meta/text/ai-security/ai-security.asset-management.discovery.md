# What the AI Asset Management console inventories show: public AI apps, AI assets, AI resources, dashboard and usage insights (ai-security.asset-management.discovery)
Also called: ai asset management discovery; public ai inventory; ai inventory

The AI Security Dashboard's AI Infrastructure tab has an Asset Explorer with AI Assets (models, MCP servers, agents, apps), AI Resources (data stores, AI software libraries, AI guardrails, identities, workloads) and Data Classification for AI tabs. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:dashboard-asset-explorer] The Dashboard's Red Teaming tab shows red-teamed AI assets by Health Score (critical, low, medium, high or unknown) and by Availability (internal or customer-facing), and lists onboarded assets not yet red teamed, whose security posture is unknown. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:dashboard-red-teaming]

**Public AI.** The Public AI Overview, Public AI Inventory - Gen AI, Endpoint AI and Embedded AI pages can each be filtered to Last 1 Day, Last 7 Days, Last 15 Days, Last Month or Last Quarter. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:public-ai-time-ranges] The Public AI Overview page (Public AI > Overview) shows Total Volume, Total Users, Upload Volume, Download Volume and Files Uploaded widgets for Gen AI apps, and a By Apps chart of the top 5 Gen AI apps with Allowed, Blocked or Isolated policy-action distribution. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:overview-widgets] On the Public AI Overview page, the By Apps chart can be filtered to Gen AI or Embedded apps and by Total Bytes, Transactions or Users, and AI Usage by Locations by Transactions or Total Bytes. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:overview-filters] On the Public AI Inventory - Gen AI page, the risk index rating scale runs from Very Low–1 to Very High–5, with Medium–3 in between. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:gen-ai-risk-index] In the Gen AI inventory list, Users counts unique authenticated users who accessed the application in the selected time range, and Locations counts unauthenticated locations from which it is accessed. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:gen-ai-users-locations] Embedded AI apps are AI-powered features built directly into popular SaaS applications, as distinct from standalone generative AI tools. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:embedded-ai-definition] On the Embedded AI inventory page, an app's Total Volume is the sum of upload and download bytes for both embedded and non-embedded AI traffic. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:embedded-ai-volume] The detail page of a Gen AI app and of an Embedded AI app has a Risk Attributes tab with Hosting Information and Security Information; its listed risk attributes include a history of data breaches in the last three years. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:risk-attributes-tab] The Endpoint AI inventory page shows the top 5 Endpoint AI apps by instances, and the count of detected application instances. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:endpoint-ai-top-apps] The Versions table on an Endpoint AI app reports Code Signing Certificate Status as Valid Signature, Certificate Expired, Unsigned, Not Valid or Invalid Signature, and Threat Type as Benign, Malicious, Suspicious or Unknown, along with the MD5 hash. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:endpoint-ai-versions]

**Usage Insights.** The Usage Insights page (Public AI > Usage Insights) has Users, Departments, Locations and Sensitive Data tabs. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:usage-insights-tabs] On Usage Insights, the Sensitive Data tab filters by Content Type (DLP Engine, DLP Dictionary or ML Category) and the Departments and Locations tabs filter by Application Type (Gen AI or Embedded AI) and policy actions Allowed, Blocked or Isolated. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:usage-insights-filters]

**Models.** The Models by Subtype widget classifies model origin as Foundation (vendor-provided pre-trained base model used as-is), Free-Tuning (foundation model trained further on the organization's data), Imported, Unknown or Others. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:models-subtypes] The Type filter on the AI Assets - Models page supports Managed (managed by a cloud service provider), Unmanaged (deployed and managed independently), Custom (built and trained in-house) and Registry Model (from a model registry or public repository). [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:models-type-filter] The Models by Risk widget rates a model's vulnerability to adversarial exploits (prompt injection, jailbreaking, unauthorized data extraction) as Critical, High, Medium or Low, and measures attack resistance rather than the severity of known open issues. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:models-risk-meaning] A benchmark-based model risk score is shown only when a benchmark has been run; the Model Security tab is visible if the model type is unmanaged and the risk score is greater than 0, and is described as only for unmanaged models. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:models-model-security-tab] The Model Security Performance Summary reports benchmark results across four quality dimensions (Security, Safety, Hallucinations, Business Alignment), with No System Prompt, Basic System Prompt and Hardened System Prompt tabs. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:models-benchmark-dimensions] For an unmanaged model, Model Supply Chain Risk gives a composite Risk Score on a scale of 0 to 100 and a Critical/High/Medium/Low severity rolled up from it; the Files check uses security scan results from Hugging Face and labels suspicious files Unsafe, Caution or Suspicious. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:models-supply-chain] The Usage and Top 10 Users tabs on a model's detail page are available only for AWS managed and custom models. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:models-usage-tabs] The AI Assets - Models and AI Assets - MCP Servers pages export their data as an Excel file with the Export button. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:models-mcp-export]

**MCP servers and agents.** An MCP server's Risk Level indicates its potential blast radius if compromised, based on command execution, file write or delete, credential access and outbound network calls; Critical means it can do all of these and Low means primarily read-only with no system-level access. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:mcp-risk-level] The Tools tab of an MCP server shows, per tool, whether it has File Access, Network Access, Code Execution and Process Access, plus the File Path it can access or targets. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:mcp-tool-fields] The MCP Servers page Type filter returns servers by whether they are remote or local, and the Asset Risk filter risk signals include execution capabilities, file deletion operations, directory traversal, external network access, credential storage/handling and processing of sensitive data. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:mcp-filters] In Zscaler AI Security, agents are AI orchestration workflows or chatbots that connect LLMs, enterprise data sources and tools (including other agents), and are treated as first-class AI assets. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:agents-definition] The Agents page describes an agent's overall risk level as rolled up from its most severe issues; the Agents by Risk widget uses Critical, High, Medium, Low or Unknown, and another widget counts agents with access to sensitive data and publicly exposed agents. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:agents-risk-widgets] On the agent detail page the Data Security tab is visible if data is filtered by sensitive data; for agents and for data stores it lists Matched Files, Triggers, DLP Engines, DLP Dictionaries, Document Categories and Document Types. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:data-security-tab]

**AI resources.** On the AI Software Libraries page a library's Status default value is Pending Review, and the Actions icon lets an admin mark a library Sanctioned or Unsanctioned to approve or reject its use. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:libraries-status-default] The Supply Chain Risk tab of an AI software library maps the overall risk score to Low 0–33, Medium 34–55, High 55–74 and Critical 75–100 (as printed; Medium and High both include 55). [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:libraries-risk-bands] A library's Total Categories is always five (Security, Dependencies, Quality, Maintenance, Compliance), and Total Issues is the number of failed checks across all categories, each failed check being one issue. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:libraries-categories] The Supply Chain Risk tab on an AI software library is visible only when a Supply Chain Risk Score is available; otherwise the tab is hidden. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:libraries-tab-visibility] The AI Software Libraries by License Type widget groups licenses into Permissive and Copyleft; the Permissive example list includes AGPL-3.0 and the Copyleft example list includes AGPL. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:libraries-license-types] On the Data Stores page, Data Stores by Risk Level reflects the severity of a data store's open security issues (None meaning no open issues), and Sensitive Data Exposed to AI counts data stores holding sensitive data (PII, PHI, PCI) connected to at least one AI model, agent or workload. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:data-stores-widgets] The graph for an AI service on the Data Stores page depicts its access path (Azure AI Foundry Hub or AWS Bedrock Knowledge Base) to the storage account containing sensitive data, and AI Security also detects whether the AI service is publicly exposed to the internet. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:data-stores-graph] AI Security detects identities in onboarded AWS, Azure and GCP cloud accounts, including managed and unmanaged databases, and Snowflake; an identity is a user, device, application or service that can access data stores containing sensitive data. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:identities-scope] The Identities article places the page at AI Infrastructure > Identities and says it shows an Access Type (with Read, Edit or Full Access as examples) and a Risk Posture (with Dormant Identity and MFA Disabled as examples) for each identity. [ai-security.asset-management.discovery:identities-fields] [divergence:ai-security.asset-management.discovery:identities-path]
