# OneAPI rate limits by product, and how the Go SDK throttles (shared.oneapi.rate-limits)
Also called: oneapi rate limit; 429 too many requests oneapi; x-ratelimit-remaining; zdx rate limit tier; cloud branch connector rate limit; business insights rate limit; Retry-After 0 seconds; rate limit headers differ per product

Each product behind the gateway publishes its own limit model. Rate limits are not one model across OneAPI: ZIA and Cloud & Branch Connector weight endpoints per method, ZPA counts per IP in 10-second windows, ZDX tiers by license count, Client Connector counts calls per hour with a daily cap on downloads, and Business Insights tiers by endpoint group. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:models-differ]

**ZIA and ZPA.** ZIA's weighted limits and headers are in [ZIA API rate limits and how the Python SDK retries](kb:zia.api.rate-limits); ZPA's per-IP limits and `retry-after` header are in [ZPA API rate limits](kb:zpa.api.rate-limits).

**ZDX.** ZDX API limits depend on the tier level by license count: tier 1 (5,000 licenses) 5 per second, 30 per minute, 1,000 per hour and 10,000 per day; tier 2 (20,000) 5, 60, 3,000 and 15,000; tier 3 (100,000) 5, 120, 6,000 and 30,000; tier 4 (more than 100,000) 5, 180, 9,000 and 60,000. Every endpoint is called from a specific IP address. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:zdx-tiers] ZDX sends the rate-limit headers `x-ratelimit-limit`, `x-ratelimit-remaining` and `x-ratelimit-reset`, the last being the seconds remaining in the current window. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:zdx-headers]

**Client Connector.** The hourly and daily limits are in [For an organization, all endpoints called from a given IP address are limited to 100 calls per hour, except `/downloadDevices`, `/downloadServiceStatus` and `/downloadDisableReasons`, which are limited to 3 calls per day.](kb:zcc.api.rate-limits:hourly-and-daily-limits). The OneAPI Client Connector page limits all endpoints to 100 calls per hour at the tenant level, except `/downloadDevices`, `/downloadServiceStatus` and `/downloadDisableReasons` at 3 calls per day, and sets `x-ratelimit-limit`, `x-ratelimit-remaining` and `x-ratelimit-reset` when limits are enforced. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:zcc-oneapi-page] The legacy page names `X-Rate-Limit-*` headers instead ([When limits are enforced the response carries `X-Rate-Limit-Remaining`, the requests left in the current window, and `X-Rate-Limit-Retry-After-Seconds`, the seconds to wait before the next call once the limit is exceeded. Exceeding the limit returns HTTP 429.](kb:zcc.api.rate-limits:headers)); which family a OneAPI 429 carries is open ([Which rate-limit headers a ZCC 429 carries on the OneAPI path](kb:zcc.sdk-python.client-configuration:oneapi-429-headers)), and whether the tenant-level OneAPI limit and the per-IP legacy limit are one bucket is open ([What shares a rate-limit bucket: credentials, auth paths, other products](kb:zcc.api.rate-limits:bucket-scope)).

**Cloud & Branch Connector.** Cloud & Branch Connector gives every endpoint a weight with a default rate limit; the typical defaults are Heavy (DELETE) 1 per minute and 40 per hour, Medium (POST, PUT) 1 per second and 400 per hour, and Light (GET) 2 per second and 1,000 per hour, and specific operations can carry a different weight. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:cbc-weights] An exceeded Cloud & Branch Connector limit returns 429 with the retry interval in the response body, for example `{"message": "Rate Limit (1/SECOND) exceeded", "Retry-After": "0 seconds"}`. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:cbc-429-body]

**Business Insights.** Business Insights sets rate limits by endpoint tier: Heavy, 1 per second and 400 per hour, for Custom Applications and Report Configurations create, get, update and delete; Light, 2 per second and 1,000 per hour, for listing and downloading reports. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:bi-tiers] An exceeded Business Insights limit returns 429 with a body carrying `errorCode` `RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `errorDetails` and `timestamp`. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:bi-429-body]

**Analytics and Event Monitoring.** The Analytics GraphQL limit is in [All Analytics API endpoints are limited to 1,000 calls per second per tenant, and responses carry `x-ratelimit-limit`, `x-ratelimit-remaining` and `x-ratelimit-reset` (seconds remaining in the window).](kb:shared.analytics.graphql-errors-and-limits:analytics-rate-limit) and the Event Monitoring limit in [All Event Monitoring endpoints are limited to 1,000 calls per second per tenant; responses carry `x-ratelimit-limit`, `x-ratelimit-remaining` and `x-ratelimit-reset`, documented here as Unix epoch seconds.](kb:shared.event-monitoring.api:rate-limit).

**Window and backoff.** For ZDX and Business Insights, every rate-limit window starts with the first call, and calls may come more than once per second within the limits. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:window-starts-first-call] The ZDX and Business Insights pages say clients that are rate limited must back off exponentially; Business Insights adds waiting for the suggested retry period on a 429. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:back-off-exponentially] The Client Connector instruction is [Clients subject to rate limits must back off exponentially to proceed further.](kb:zcc.api.rate-limits:back-off-exponentially). The ZIA and Cloud & Branch Connector pages recommend a wait (sleep) period in scripts after each call to an endpoint. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:sleep-between-calls] The Business Insights page also recommends tracking API usage, planning Custom Applications and Report Configurations operations to minimize calls, and caching report listings and configuration data. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:bi-best-practices]

**Go SDK throttling.** The Go SDK's OneAPI client throttles on the client side with a limiter per service: ZIA and ZTW, each at 20 GET and 10 POST/PUT/DELETE calls per 10 seconds. At v3.8.55 both had hourly caps of 950 GET, 950 POST/PUT and 380 DELETE per method; at v3.8.58 ZIA's hourly caps are per endpoint (method plus path, IDs normalized) at 950 GET, 380 POST/PUT and 380 DELETE, while ZTW keeps the per-method 950, 950 and 380. ZPA has 20 GET and 10 POST/PUT/DELETE calls per 10 seconds. ZCC and ZDX each get a limiter built as `NewRateLimiter(100, 3, 3600, 86400)`, whose arguments set 100 GET calls per 3,600 seconds and 3 POST/PUT/DELETE calls per 86,400 seconds whatever the path; its comment says 'downloadDevices: 3 per day', but `DownloadDevices` sends a GET. ZIdentity paths (prefix `ziam`), `/admin` paths and any path no service prefix matches use a default limiter, `NewRateLimiter(2, 1, 1, 1)`. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:go-client-limiters] The 86,400 seconds there is not the window the limiter holds: [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:zcc-zdx-daily-write-cap-is-hourly] The Go SDK builds its ZDX limiter with the same arguments as the ZCC one, commented as 100 calls per hour and 3 per day for downloadDevices. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:go-zdx-limiter-copies-zcc] Before it checks any limit, the Go `RateLimiter.Wait` removes every recorded request older than one hour, the combined POST/PUT/DELETE list included; it then compares the recorded writes with the write limit and, when the limit is reached and the oldest recorded write is younger than `postPutDeleteFreq` seconds, returns a wait of `postPutDeleteFreq` seconds less that write's age. A limiter built with `NewRateLimiter` takes this path for every request whatever the path, because `WaitFor` hands a limiter without per-endpoint hourly limits to `Wait(method)`, and the transport sleeps for the returned wait plus any configured additional delay, or until the request's context is done, then asks the limiter again and sends the request only once the limiter admits it. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:go-limiter-forgets-after-an-hour] So the ZCC and ZDX limiters, built for 3 writes per 86,400 seconds, keep a write on record for one hour only: a write is admitted at once when fewer than three writes were recorded in the past hour, so more than three writes a day pass when they are spread over hours, while a fourth write within an hour of the oldest of three is told to wait 86,400 seconds less that write's age, more than 23 hours. [shared.oneapi.rate-limits:zcc-zdx-daily-write-cap-is-hourly] How long such a wait actually lasts is open [gap:shared.oneapi.rate-limits:limiter-wait-vs-client-timeout]. How the SDK retries a 429 is in [How the Go SDK retries requests (and replays writes)](kb:shared.sdk-go.retries).

What to check:
1. Which product's limit model and header names apply before reading remaining-quota headers, since they differ by product.
2. For Cloud & Branch Connector deletes, whether the run stays within 1 per minute and 40 per hour.
3. For a Go SDK client calling ZDX, whether a client-side throttle (not a server 429) is what slowed the run.
4. Serialize concurrent jobs against one product where possible. Whether they share one budget is open.
5. Whether a persistent 429 is cleared by a rate-limit increase is open; do not assume it is.

Inferred, not quoted (say so when an answer rests on one):

ZCC and ZDX GETs (100 per 3,600 seconds) are unaffected, since their window equals the one-hour cleanup. How long the transport actually sleeps is not settled here: getHTTPClient gives the OneAPI HTTP client a 60-second timeout by default, and whether net/http puts that deadline on the request context that `sleepCtx` watches is standard-library behaviour no captured source states (gap limiter-wait-vs-client-timeout).
