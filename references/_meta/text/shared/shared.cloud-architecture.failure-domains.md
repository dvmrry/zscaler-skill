# What happens when a Zscaler component fails: edges, Central Authority, cloud, feeds, logs (shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains)
Also called: blast radius; zscaler outage; what happens when the central authority fails; service edge down; nanolog outage; feed central outage; cloud unreachable
Former IDs: shared-20

The architecture articles describe redundancy for each component; fewer sources say what a failure looks like from the tenant's side. This subject collects what is stated and what is open, per component.

**One Public Service Edge.** [Public Service Edges are always deployed in active-active load balancing mode worldwide, and the Central Authority monitors their health to ensure availability.](kb:shared.cloud-architecture.service-edges:active-active) How fast traffic moves to another edge, and who moves it, is open [gap:shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:edge-failover-timing].

**A Central Authority server or node.** [The two Central Authorities use different availability models: ZIA runs one active server with two passive standbys that can take over, while every ZPA CA node actively services requests. A ZIA CA failover is a standby promotion; ZPA has no standby to promote.](kb:shared.cloud-architecture.central-authority:different-models) If a network failure or another event keeps a ZIA Public Service Edge from reaching or downloading configuration from the CA, the edge switches immediately to Safe mode: it enforces all cached policies, logs user access for user and location configurations already in its cache, and tries to reconnect to the CA every second, leaving Safe mode as soon as a healthy connection is restored. [shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:safe-mode] In Safe mode full security inspection is still enforced; a request whose user or location policy cannot be downloaded gets a default URL policy that blocks the Legal Liability URL category, and no authentication is requested. [shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:safe-mode-default-policy] Whether a standby promotion triggers Safe mode, and how long Safe mode can last, is open [gap:shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:ca-outage-traffic-impact]; the Private Access side of that question is [Policy caching and Central Authority reconnect behavior of the Public tier](kb:zpa.public-service-edges.what-it-is:ca-reconnect-semantics).

**The whole cloud, for a tenant.** [The service mainly helps organizations stay connected and protected during critical failure scenarios in which the public clouds are unreachable.](kb:zia.private-service-edge.business-continuity:trigger-is-critical-failure) [Only the last known good policy configuration applies to outgoing traffic in business continuity mode: recent policy updates might not take effect immediately, and new user authentication or enrollment is not supported.](kb:zia.private-service-edge.business-continuity:last-known-good-policy) On the client, [The app may need to disable its Internet Security service and let users reach the internet directly in three situations: a captive portal requires payment or acceptance before connecting, the app cannot reach ZIA Public Service Edges, or it cannot set up its Z-Tunnel.](kb:zcc.forwarding-profiles.fail-open:situations) Which of the two acts first when both are configured is open [gap:shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:bc-vs-fail-open-order].

**Tunnels in business continuity.** [Zscaler Tunnel 2.0 is not supported in business continuity mode.](kb:zia.private-service-edge.business-continuity:z-tunnel-2-unsupported) What a Z-Tunnel 2.0 client does at that point is open [gap:shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:bc-ztunnel2-fallback], and so is how many business continuity sites exist and which one a tenant uses [gap:shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:bc-site-coverage].

**Feed Central.** [Feed Central distributes its threat intelligence and other feeds to the Central Authority, which sends updates to the Public Service Edges, so that every Public Service Edge has the latest URL database and malware and threat information.](kb:shared.cloud-architecture.support-systems:feed-path) What edges do with their feeds while Feed Central is unreachable is open [gap:shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:feed-central-outage].

**A Nanolog cluster.** [Each Nanolog cluster has one active server and two passive standbys; the active Nanolog replicates data to the other two immediately, so any can become active with no data loss, and each server is hosted in a separate location.](kb:shared.cloud-architecture.logging-path:nanolog-topology) What a cluster outage does to logs in flight and to NSS or LSS streams is open [gap:shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:nanolog-outage]; Cloud NSS can replay up to an hour: [Cloud NSS delivery reads from the Nanolog: after an interruption, the Cloud NSS one-hour recovery lets the Nanolog replay logs up to one hour back.](kb:zia.logs.nss-feed-format:cloud-from-nanolog).

**Customer-side failover.** What happens to a live session when the customer's own failover moves it to another edge is open [gap:shared.cloud-architecture.failure-domains:source-ip-change-mid-session]; tunnel failover design is in [GRE tunnels to ZIA: requirements, bandwidth and deployment](kb:zia.traffic-forwarding.gre).

What to check:
1. Name the component and the product before describing an outage; for a CA, ZIA promotes a standby and ZPA has none.
2. For ZIA users who lost access or saw Legal Liability blocks with no authentication prompt during a cloud incident, consider Safe mode on the edge.
3. For a whole-cloud outage, check whether the tenant subscribes to the Business Continuity Cloud and which fail-open settings its forwarding profiles carry.
