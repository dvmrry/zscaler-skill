# Writing SSL/TLS inspection exemptions without exempting too much (zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions)
Also called: ssl bypass rules; ssl exemption hygiene; do not inspect rule design; s3 wildcard exemption; ssl inspection exceptions; click-to-run exemption; audit ssl bypass

An exemption is a rule that opts traffic out of the opt-out default.

**Whether to exempt.** Zscaler strongly recommends inspecting all traffic and placing a bypass only for controlled, understood exceptions, and only after consulting legal counsel for the impacted region. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:bypass-only-controlled] In general, bypasses are applicable only for specific functions, such as banking and finance destinations, healthcare destinations, business functions that need undecryptable traffic, business functions that need certificate-pinned sites or applications, and applications such as parts of Microsoft 365 that break when inspected. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:bypass-purposes] Some organizations bypass the Finance and Health URL categories for privacy reasons, but Zscaler recommends enabling inspection for all URL categories. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:finance-health-recommendation] Build the rule set as opt-out: end with an Inspect rule for everything not explicitly exempted, so new groups, applications, categories and locations are inspected automatically until configured otherwise. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:opt-out-design] The deployment best practices say to enable SSL/TLS Inspection on a small location or test lab before enabling it on all locations, to start by enabling it for risky URL categories only, such as Privacy Risk and Legal Liability, and to include all other categories in the list of URL categories for which SSL/TLS transactions are not to be decrypted. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:phased-rollout-start] The rollout's final step excepts Finance and Health, which differs from the other pages [divergence:zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:finance-health-rollout].

**Which variant.** The Evaluate and Bypass variants of Do Not Inspect are not interchangeable; Bypass also skips URL Filtering and Cloud App Control, and Zscaler advises against it for custom exemptions ([Where SSL inspection sits in ZIA web policy evaluation (the two passes)](kb:zia.ssl-inspection.pipeline)). In the guide's example rule set, the Zscaler Recommended Exemption rule is Do Not Inspect with Evaluate Other Policies, so URL Filtering still runs, while the Zscaler Microsoft 365 Click-to-Run rule is Do Not Inspect with Bypass Other Policies, so it does not. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:predefined-exemptions-differ] The guide's Microsoft 365 policy is to inspect OneDrive, SharePoint and similar services but exclude Click-to-Run. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:m365-inspect-except-click-to-run]

**How to write the entry.** Bypass entries should be FQDNs or domains, not IP addresses. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:fqdn-not-ip] Prefer the most specific domains over wildcards: add `corp.safemarch.com` and `eng.safemarch.com` rather than `.safemarch.com`. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:specific-not-wildcard] A bypass entry must be the FQDN only: anything beyond the domain, such as a path, is not visible without first inspecting the communication. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:no-paths] Where a matching Cloud Application exists, use it instead of managing the domains by hand. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:prefer-cloud-app] Keep SSL exceptions in at least one dedicated URL category or destination group, don't create bypasses to resolve access issues without proper investigation, and document every entry for later audit. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:exception-hygiene]

**The wildcard that exempts everyone.** Avoid exempting the shared-storage CDN domains `.s3.amazonaws.com`, `.blob.core.windows.net`, `.cloudfront.net` or similar major CDNs without serious consideration: any subscriber can get its own storage subdomain there, so exempting the parent leaves every AWS S3 or Azure Blob storage account uninspected. [zia.ssl-inspection.exemptions:shared-storage-domains] The same shape of mistake exists under transparent forwarding with the Miscellaneous or Unknown category; that is covered in [Where SSL inspection sits in ZIA web policy evaluation (the two passes)](kb:zia.ssl-inspection.pipeline).

Rule order decides whether an exemption wins ([SSL/TLS inspection rule order, predefined and default rules, limits](kb:zia.ssl-inspection.rules)). What the exempted traffic loses is in [What stops working when traffic bypasses SSL/TLS inspection](kb:zia.ssl-inspection.bypass-impact). Populations that cannot be inspected anyway are in [What SSL/TLS inspection cannot inspect: pinned apps, client certs, QUIC, BYOD, IoT](kb:zia.ssl-inspection.uninspectable).
