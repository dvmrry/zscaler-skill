# China Premium and Premium Plus Internet Access: service model, provisioning, backup, licensing and DNS (zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium)
Also called: China Premium Access; China Premium Plus; ZIA China Premium; internet access in mainland China; Great Firewall

China Premium Internet Access provides internet access for users residing in mainland China, including access to international sites and applications such as Salesforce and Microsoft 365. China Premium for Private Access is covered in a separate article. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:what] China Premium Access is based on the Chinese technology partners' IP backbone, which passes all domestically and internationally bound traffic through the Great Firewall; the partners have registered all IP addresses assigned to the service under China's Internet Content Provider (ICP) scheme on Zscaler's behalf. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:premium-backbone] China Premium Plus connections use a dedicated cross-border private link that the Chinese technology partners register with the relevant government agencies via China Telecom, China Unicom and China Mobile; the page says the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) accepts VPNs for cross-border communications and connections to outside mainland China for business purposes. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:plus-backbone] For both tiers, ensuring compliance with all applicable Chinese laws and regulations when using the Zscaler service is ultimately the responsibility of the end user customer. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:customer-compliance]

**Service model.** The Managing Internet & SaaS Use in China page says China Premium uses a Private Service Edge for Internet & SaaS hosted at a partner site within China with a premium Chinese ISP, and that IPSec or GRE tunnels can be established from any endpoint to a Private Service Edge managed by Zscaler partners in China, used similarly to a public Zscaler data center; China Premium Plus uses a Private Service Edge hosted at a partner site within China whose premium ISP has a dedicated link to international sites. For China Premium, traffic can be broken and inspected within China, the partner premium fabric serves any destination, there is no traffic forwarding limitation, and Zscaler manages the partner premium fabric. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:partner-pse] The Deploying Zscaler Internet Access in China page says Zscaler's partnership with service providers in China includes deploying ZIA Public Service Edge data centers within China and ZIA Private Service Edge devices within the service providers themselves; the guide is delivered as a PDF download. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:partnership-deployment] Who operates the China Premium data centers, and which kind of Service Edge they are, is described differently across the pages [divergence:zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:china-edge-operator]. The Managing Internet & SaaS Use in China page says China Premium Plus is best suited for organizations with the highest bandwidth and user counts. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:plus-fit] The page's benefit list for China Premium includes shared premium bandwidth, international traffic routed via the Great Firewall, Z-Tunnel 2.0 for any traffic and any port, and no traffic split at the branch offices; the list for China Premium Plus includes dedicated international bandwidth, a bandwidth control option, optional content filtering, Z-Tunnel 2.0 for any traffic and any port, and no traffic split at the branch offices. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:benefits]

**Enabling and provisioning.** To enable either tier, the organization purchases the service, has Zscaler provision it, and limits bandwidth at the source or with Zscaler bandwidth controls. Provisioning starts with a Zscaler Support ticket giving the source tunnel IP address to the China POP for GRE; Zscaler associates the virtual IP address with the service and makes the required changes to the Public Service Edge for Internet & SaaS within 48 hours (the China Premium section's wording), and the bandwidth limit of the IPSec or GRE tunnel is set so it does not exceed the purchased bandwidth. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:enable-and-provision] The IPSec provisioning steps are open [gap:zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:ipsec-provisioning].

**Backup.** In the China Premium backup section, Beijing and Shanghai data centers are available and either can be the primary based on users' locations. Either can be requested, and Zscaler reviews the situation and assigns an appropriate data center. Remote users' PAC files define the subcloud for both the Beijing and Shanghai data centers so users connect to the closest one. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:backup-dcs] In the China Premium backup section, the bandwidth purchased for China Premium must not be exceeded across both data centers: for example, 100 Mbps in Beijing means the overall bandwidth for Shanghai and Beijing must not exceed 100 Mbps. Backup bandwidth can be purchased only with China Premium service, and China Premium Plus backup bandwidth only with China Premium Plus service. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:backup-bandwidth]

**Licensing and fair use.** ZIA China Premium and ZIA China Premium Plus are SaaS products licensed according to the bandwidth purchased for the subscription term in an order. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:licensing-basis] For China Premium, bandwidth is the data transmitted and processed by ZIA within a Premium data center in China. It is calculated in Mbps from five-minute samples of received (Rx) and transmitted (Tx) traffic. For each data center, a calendar month's samples are sorted and the 95th percentile is taken (P95 Accounting Method), separately for Tx and Rx with the larger used as that data center's usage; the P95 values of all Premium data centers accessed in the month are summed to give the month's total. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:premium-p95] For China Premium, usage must stay within the purchased bandwidth 95% of the time in any calendar month. Because usage is measured at the 95th percentile, it can burst above the purchased bandwidth for up to 5% of the month at no additional charge, approximately 36 hours in a 30-day month. A burst may never exceed 130% of the purchased bandwidth, and excess bursting, in time or in bandwidth, is best-effort and may be throttled down to the purchased bandwidth. In the page's three examples with 140 Mbps purchased, a 200 Mbps peak at 5% burst duration is not within limits because the peak exceeds 130%, 180 Mbps at 5% is within limits, and 170 Mbps at 6% is not because the duration exceeds 5% of five-minute samples. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:premium-burst] For China Premium Plus, bandwidth is the data transmitted and processed by ZIA within a Premium Plus data center in China, measured with the Peak Accounting Method: the highest five-minute sample per data center in a calendar month, with Tx and Rx calculated separately and the larger used, and the values of all Premium Plus data centers summed. The bursting limitations applicable to China Premium do not apply to China Premium Plus. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:plus-peak] For both China Premium and China Premium Plus: if usage exceeds the total purchased bandwidth the customer must promptly reduce it or order more; Zscaler reserves the right to deploy bandwidth control policies to hold consumption to the purchased bandwidth; Zscaler may use reports from third-party network and internet service providers as well as internal tools when calculating consumption; on written request Zscaler provides bandwidth usage reports for the previous calendar month; and use is expected to follow the Acceptable Use Policy. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:overage-enforcement] Use of both China Premium and China Premium Plus is subject to Zscaler's Supplemental Premium China Services Availability Agreement. For China Premium, the Service Availability Agreement does not apply if usage exceeds the burst limits: greater than 130% at any time and/or greater than 5% of five-minute samples in a calendar month. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:availability-agreement] The agreement's terms are open [gap:zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:availability-agreement-terms].

**Health360.** Bandwidth utilization for the premium services can be viewed on Health360. Health360 Service Health gives a near-real-time overview of China Premium connectivity, showing current and historic bandwidth utilization against purchased bandwidth. On the Service Health: China Connectivity page (Analytics > Health360 > China Connectivity) an admin can show the last 24 hours, 48 hours, 7 days, 30 days or a custom range, choose a data center, export to CSV, and switch between China Premium and Premium Plus. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:health360] The Premium view shows total bandwidth usage for Internet & SaaS and Private Access and the number of times usage exceeded the allowed limits. Its Internet Access Bandwidth Usage graph shows total inbound and outbound Internet & SaaS traffic across all points of presence within China for the filtered time period, and an exceeded bandwidth limit is shown in red. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:health360-premium] The Premium Plus view shows the total bandwidth usage and the number of times usage exceeded the allowed limits for Internet & SaaS and Private Access, with two graphs: International, for Internet & SaaS traffic routing through China international links, and Domestic, for Internet & SaaS traffic routing within mainland China. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:health360-plus] What the exceeded limit is measured against is open [gap:zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:health360-limit].

**DNS.** To access websites with DNS issues, DNS traffic must be forwarded to Zscaler. If the issue has been identified as DNS poisoning, Zscaler needs time to change the policy, a maximum of three days. [zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:dns-poisoning]

What to check:
1. Which tier the organization bought, since Premium bandwidth is measured at P95 with a burst cap and Premium Plus at the peak sample with no Premium burst limits.
2. Who operates the data center and which kind of Service Edge it is before deciding whom to contact in an outage, since the pages differ.

Sources disagree (name both sides):
- The China Premium pages disagree on who operates the China Premium data centers and what kind of Service Edge they are: operated by Zscaler, a Private Service Edge managed by partners, or both Public and Private Service Edges. [divergence:zia.traffic-forwarding.china-premium:china-edge-operator]
  - zscaler-help: The licensing page defines China Premium bandwidth as data processed within a Premium data center operated by Zscaler in China.
  - zscaler-help: The Managing page has tunnels going to a Private Service Edge managed by Zscaler partners in China, hosted at a partner site.
  - zscaler-help: The same page's provisioning step has Zscaler making the changes to the Public Service Edge for Internet & SaaS.
  - zscaler-help: The deployment guide names both ZIA Public Service Edge data centers within China and ZIA Private Service Edge devices within the service providers.
  Impact: Both could be true, with a partner hosting and managing the Private Service Edge while Zscaler runs the service on it, but no page says so; the answer decides whom to contact in an outage and which kind of Service Edge's behavior applies.
