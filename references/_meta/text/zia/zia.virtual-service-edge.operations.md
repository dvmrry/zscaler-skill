# Who runs a Virtual Service Edge: access, logging, hardening and monitoring (zia.virtual-service-edge.operations)
Also called: vse zscaler access; vse support tunnel; vse auth.log; vse hardening; vse snmp; vse logs; vse default password; vse jailed environment; vse monitoring
Former IDs: zia-35

**Who is responsible.** The organization has full access to its Virtual Service Edges for monitoring and configuration, Zscaler does not require access, and the Virtual Service Edges are not monitored or managed by the Zscaler Cloud Operations team. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:customer-access-zscaler-not-required] Two support-tunnel options exist per instance, both disabled by default: Zscaler Initiated On-Demand Support Tunnel lets Zscaler establish a support tunnel whenever required (and greys out the Admin Console's Establish Support Tunnel), and Establish Support Tunnel lets the service establish a tunnel for Zscaler Support to access the instance. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:support-tunnel-options] So "Zscaler cannot get in" is the default, and it changes if either support-tunnel option is switched on. The hardware form factor is the opposite model: Zscaler runs it ([What a ZIA Private Service Edge is, who runs it, and what it is not](kb:zia.private-service-edge.what-it-is)). Upgrades happen automatically, with timing controls: [How Virtual Service Edges and their clusters upgrade](kb:zia.virtual-service-edge.upgrades).

The maintenance support article describes a different operating model [divergence:zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:who-manages-vse].

**What it shares with the cloud.** Policies are defined once in the Admin Console and apply whether a user connects to an on-premises Virtual Service Edge or to a Public Service Edge anywhere in the world. Logs are transmitted to and stored on the Zscaler cloud as the central repository for analytics. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:policy-and-logs-in-cloud] Whether a log entry names the VM or the cluster is not documented ([Whether log entries carry a VM-level or cluster-level identifier](kb:zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:log-identifier)).

**Hardening and local logs.** The appliance is locked down to a limited set of commands (a jailed environment) on a hardened operating system; CVEs are patched immediately if they can cause harm, and updates and patches are downloaded and applied automatically. Access is limited to the VMware console, Microsoft Azure web console view and Hyper-V Manager console, and the management interface for SSH. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:hardened-appliance] Authentication attempts, and an audit log of the commands run on the appliance, are stored in /var/log/auth.log and can be monitored remotely or checked periodically to detect unauthorized commands or authentication attempts. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:auth-log] A new VM boots with default console credentials (zsroot) and prompts for a password change at first login; direct root login is not permitted and administrators use sudo. Running `vzen enforce-password-complexity` optionally enforces password complexity. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:first-login] Treat /var/log/auth.log as the place to look for unauthorized access to the appliance; whether any copy leaves the VM is not stated ([Whether /var/log/auth.log is sent to the Zscaler cloud](kb:zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:auth-log-transmission)).

**Monitoring.** Virtual Service Edges support SNMPv3 only; the SNMP user accepted is the one configured with `sudo vzen snmp-admin-configure`, with MD5 or SHA1 authentication and DES or AES encryption, and traps are configured with `sudo vzen snmp-trap-configure`. The MIB article's example uses SNMP version 3 with security level auth, priv, and the Zscaler MIBs sit under iso.org.dod.internet > private > enterprises > zscaler in the MIB tree. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:snmpv3-only] The Zscaler Enterprise SNMP MIBs are downloadable files that define all counters and assign them within the Zscaler database; they can be used for health monitoring of instances such as the Nanolog Streaming Service (NSS) and the Virtual Service Edge. The Virtual Service Edge bundle, VZENMIBS.tgz, contains ZSCALER-OSNIC-MIB, ZSCALER-PROCESSHEALTH-MIB, ZSCALER-PROCESSWATCHDOG-MIB, ZSCALER-ROLEHEALTH-MIB, ZSCALER-SWAPINFO-MIB, ZSCALER-VSE-MIB and ZSCALER-ZSCALERNIC-MIB. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:snmp-mibs] ZSCALER-VSE-MIB describes the health of the Virtual Service Edge's connection to the Zscaler Central Authority and to the Log and Reporting servers. ZSCALER-ROLEHEALTH-MIB holds instance health (OS-level information such as CPU and memory, plus some instance-specific information); ZSCALER-PROCESSHEALTH-MIB covers all watched processes and ZSCALER-PROCESSWATCHDOG-MIB the watchdog information for each managed process; ZSCALER-SWAPINFO-MIB covers the swap devices; and ZSCALER-OSNIC-MIB and ZSCALER-ZSCALERNIC-MIB cover the NIC devices managed by the OS and by the Zscaler instance respectively. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:mib-modules] The NSS bundle is in [The NSS MIB bundle, NSSMIBS.tgz, contains ZSCALER-NSSFEED-MIB (information specific to NSS feeds) and ZSCALER-NSS-MIB (information specific to the NSS instance) along with ZSCALER-OSNIC-MIB, ZSCALER-PROCESSHEALTH-MIB, ZSCALER-PROCESSWATCHDOG-MIB, ZSCALER-ROLEHEALTH-MIB, ZSCALER-SWAPINFO-MIB and ZSCALER-ZSCALERNIC-MIB. The MIB article says the steps for configuring NSS are identical to its Virtual Service Edge example, and to use the nss status command for NSS.](kb:shared.nss.vm-operations:nss-snmp-mibs). The MIB article gives no Zscaler enterprise OID or trap configuration [gap:zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:mib-oids-and-traps]. Beyond SNMP and the Admin Console lists, what health view exists for a Virtual Service Edge is not documented ([Health visibility for a Virtual Service Edge](kb:zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:vse-health-visibility)); the ZIA PSE Health Dashboard needs ZDX and is described for Private Service Edges.

**The cluster monitoring page.** The cluster monitoring page says that if a GRE tunnel or Layer 2 forwarding is configured from the router to a Virtual Service Edge, IPSLAs can be enabled to monitor the tunnels. Virtual Service Edges support NET-SNMP, and a management system that supports SNMPv3 can monitor a cluster; the page sets snmpd_enable="YES" (by default this entry is set to NO) and snmpd_conffile="/usr/local/etc/snmpd.conf". [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:snmp-agent] Whether this is the same agent that `vzen snmp-admin-configure` configures is not stated [gap:zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:snmp-agent-enablement]. The monitoring page's VSE-MIB::ca_connectivity (.1.3.6.1.4.1.46262.9.2.1.5) is set to 3 if the connection is established, with 0 not authenticated, 1 authentication bypassed, 2 authentication bypassed with probe and 4 down; VSE-MIB::smsm_connectivity (.1.3.6.1.4.1.46262.9.2.1.6) is set to 4 or 5 if the connection is established, with 2 initiated, 3 waiting for the logging server, 6 closing and 7 closed. A CPU usage percentage (VSE-MIB::cpu_percentage) below 90% is acceptable and above it an alert must be triggered, and likewise for swap memory usage (VSE-MIB::swapinfo) at 50%. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:vse-mib] The OIDs that generate SNMP alert traps by default are: the SME going down and the link to the Zscaler cloud becoming inactive; more than 1 mountd process; ntalkd processes out of range (0-4) or sendmail processes out of range (1-10); the root partition having less than 10 MBs of space or any other partition less than 10%; the load average crossing 3 for 1 minute, 2 for 5 minutes or 1 for 15 minutes; and CPU usage beyond 90%. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:default-traps]

**Health page.** The Zscaler service performs ICMP and HTTP monitoring from the load balancer to the Virtual Service Edge to monitor instance health. With serv_port=3128 in the SME section of the configuration, curl http://<Virtual Service Edge IP>:3128/index.html?=100 returns a 200 OK and a payload size of 100 bytes, which does not include ICMP monitoring. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:http-health-page]

**Time.** Virtual Service Edges are extremely sensitive to the VM and cloud times being in sync. By default the VM synchronizes the time with public NTP servers (for example pool.ntp.org); `sudo vzen enable-ntpd` and the vzen disable-ntpd command start and stop configured NTP servers, and the CLI adds or removes custom servers without syntax errors or duplicate entries, restarting the Virtual Service Edge and NTP service. Adding alternate NTP servers to /etc/ntp.conf is not supported; the page offers two alternatives for a local NTP server: a cron entry running ntpdate every 10 minutes, or /etc/hosts entries (up to 4) that make the pool.ntp.org host names resolve to the local NTP server IP address. [zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:ntp]

The vzen commands for reading state are in [The vzen command line for configuring and troubleshooting a Virtual Service Edge](kb:zia.virtual-service-edge.troubleshooting).

Sources disagree (name both sides):
- Who manages and maintains a Virtual Service Edge. [divergence:zia.virtual-service-edge.operations:who-manages-vse]
  - zscaler-help: The About page says Zscaler does not require access and Cloud Operations does not monitor or manage the Virtual Service Edges.
  - zscaler-help: The comparison page says the organization is responsible for deployment, configuration and maintenance, though upgrades are automatic.
  - zscaler-help: The maintenance support page says Zscaler Cloud Operations manages and maintains the Virtual Service Edges, only Zscaler staff should access or service them, and the organization monitors them with SNMP MIBs and keeps someone able to respond 24/7 to Zscaler.
  Impact: The pages describe opposite operating models, customer-run with no Zscaler access against Zscaler-managed with Zscaler-only access, so who patches, accesses and monitors a Virtual Service Edge cannot be answered from one page; the maintenance page's duties are not merged into this subject's claims.
