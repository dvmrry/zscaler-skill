# PRA Privileged Desktops: AWS IAM policy and cross-account role templates (zpa.pra.privileged-desktops)
Also called: privileged desktops; pra privileged desktop; zpa-privileged-desktops; DJB-Cross-Account-Role; disposable jump box; ZPA-PRA-Cross-Account-Policy; pra aws cloudformation; pra cross account role

Privileged Desktops is a PRA feature whose AWS side is prepared with two CloudFormation templates from the zscaler/zpa-privileged-desktops repository. The user-facing feature is not described by a captured help page [gap:zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:desktop-feature]; see [Privileged Remote Access: what it is and where each question goes](kb:zpa.pra) for PRA itself.

**Purpose.** The zscaler/zpa-privileged-desktops repository is described as the repo for the Privileged Desktop feature of Privileged Remote Access (PRA), holding CloudFormation templates that establish the trust relationship and IAM role. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:repo-purpose] The repository has two CloudFormation templates: permissions_policy_step_1.yml creates a customer-managed IAM policy and create_iam_role_and_trust_step_2.yml creates a cross-account role with a trust policy. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:two-steps] The step 1 policy is described as a customer-managed IAM policy for ZPA Privileged Remote Access (PRA) that grants the necessary permissions for creating and managing disposable jump boxes and other resources. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:policy-purpose]

**Step 1, the policy.** The step 1 template takes TargetAccountId (the AWS account where resources will be managed) and TargetRegion (the AWS Region) as parameters, names the policy ZPA-PRA-Cross-Account-Policy-<TargetRegion>, and outputs PolicyArn. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:policy-parameters] The step 1 policy lets PRA create a VPC, subnets, route tables, an internet gateway and security groups only when the Name tag in the request matches *ZPA-PRA*, and delete a VPC or an internet gateway only when its Name tag matches *ZPA-PRA*. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:network-scope] The step 1 policy allows ec2:RunInstances for instances only with a Name tag that starts with ZPA-PRA, and allows ec2:ModifyInstanceAttribute and ec2:TerminateInstances only on instances whose Name tag starts with ZPA-PRA. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:instance-scope] The step 1 policy grants a set of EC2 describe actions plus ec2:CreateTags, ec2:CreateKeyPair and cloudformation:DescribeStacks on Resource "*", and cloudformation:CreateStack, cloudformation:DeleteStack and cloudformation:DescribeStackEvents on any stack in the target account and region. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:untagged-scope]

**Step 2, the role.** The step 2 template takes TrustedPrincipals, a comma-separated list of AWS principal ARNs allowed to assume the role, and ManagedPolicyArnsList, a comma-separated list of managed policy ARNs to attach, and outputs RoleArn and RoleId. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:role-parameters] The role is named DJB-Cross-Account-Role in the step 2 template, and the step 1 policy grants iam:GetRole and iam:ListAttachedRolePolicies on the role of that name in the target account. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:role-name-fixed] The role's trust policy allows the TrustedPrincipals to call sts:AssumeRole with an empty Condition ({}), so the template adds no further condition such as an external ID. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:trust-policy] The step 1 PolicyArn output is the managed policy to supply to the step 2 ManagedPolicyArnsList parameter. [zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:policy-then-role] Which principals to trust and how the role is registered are open [gap:zpa.pra.privileged-desktops:principal-and-registration].

What to check:
1. Before creating the stacks, which account and region the policy targets, since the policy name carries the region.
2. Whether the permissions requested match what the security team will accept: tag-scoped for network and instances, unscoped for describe, key pair and stack actions.
3. Whether the trust policy needs an external ID or other condition, which the template leaves empty.
