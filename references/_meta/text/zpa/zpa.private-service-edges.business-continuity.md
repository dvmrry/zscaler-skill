# Business Continuity for Private Access: prerequisites, domain names, settings, entering and exiting, limitations (zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity)
Also called: business continuity settings; business continuity domain; entering business continuity; exiting business continuity; business continuity sni; business continuity fqdn

Business Continuity for Private Access lets users keep accessing applications during Private Access-related cloud outages or Internet Service Provider outages, without any manual intervention. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:purpose]

**Prerequisites.** To ensure that Business Continuity functions properly, Private Cloud Controllers must run on the same or a later software version as the App Connectors and Private Service Edges. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:prereq-version] The Understanding Business Continuity page expects that end users' machines run Client Connector 4.6 or later for Windows; that Private Cloud Controllers receive inbound connections from end users, App Connectors and Private Service Edges on port 443; that end users resolve the Business Continuity FQDNs and reach the Private Cloud Controllers and Private Service Edges using the Business Continuity SNIs on port 443; that App Connectors and Private Service Edges resolve the FQDNs and connect outbound to the Private Cloud Controllers on port 443; and that the Private Service Edge receives inbound connections from end users and all App Connectors on port 443. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:prereq-network] All applications and all access policies must be configured before entering Business Continuity. Customers must provide DNS servers and a per-tenant identity provider to be used during Business Continuity; a SIEM for visibility or logging, and GPO or Mobile Device Management installation of Client Connector with customer-specific configurations for new user enrollment, are optional infrastructure changes. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:prereq-config] The page's Client Connector Portal steps are to enable Business Continuity per app profile, to sync, download and copy the thumbprint of the Business Continuity configuration, and to deploy Client Connector with the Business Continuity installation parameters. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:client-connector-portal-steps]

**Names it uses.** Business Continuity requires DNS servers hosted in the organization's infrastructure to resolve the FQDNs. Zscaler provides a Business Continuity domain (for example tenant_name.zpa.zsbcs.net). [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:dns-and-managed-domain] The settings page gives example names for the initial DNS request to get the Private Cloud Controller IP addresses (any2bcp.<business_continuity_domain>), for the TLS connection to the Private Cloud Controller for identity provider redirection (bcpsp.<business_continuity_domain>) and for the TLS connection for the SAML request (bcpsp-<pcc gid>.<business_continuity_domain>). [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:fqdns-client-connector] The DNS request to get the Private Cloud Controller's IP address is co2bcp.<business_continuity_domain> for App Connectors and pb2bcp.<business_continuity_domain> for Private Service Edges for Private Access; based on the DNS resolution, the Private Service Edge or App Connectors establish a TLS connection to the Private Cloud Controller. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:fqdns-components] Client Connector's TLS connections to the Private Cloud Controller and to the Private Service Edge both use the SNI c2.<business_continuity_domain> for enrolled users and c2site.<business_continuity_domain> for new users. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:snis] A Business Continuity SP FQDN is generated per Private Cloud Controller for the authentication of users in Business Continuity (for example bcpsp-<pccgid>.<business_continuity_domain>). Changing the Business Continuity Domain initiates a new enrollment process for Private Cloud Controllers and updates the SP Assertion Consumer Service URL and Entity ID. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:domain-change-reenrolls]

**Settings.** The New User Enrollment setting is enabled to enroll new users during Business Continuity; mutual TLS authentication between users and Private Service Edges is not supported for new user enrollment during Business Continuity. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:new-user-enrollment] Configuration Backup is enabled to back up configuration data used by App Connectors and Private Service Edges to an SQLite database, configuration files (image snapshots, metadata and version number) and configuration snapshots (cryptographic and configuration files) used by the Private Cloud Controller; by default it is disabled. The default is 15 backups, and a maximum of 100 backups can be created. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:configuration-backup] The Backup Path is where the Private Cloud Controller stores backups of database and configuration files. A new drive must be mounted, or an NFS/SMB must be shared for the backup path, and if the backup path is changed it must be owned by zscaler:zscaler. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:backup-path] Backup Frequency is Hourly, Daily or Weekly, Daily by default. For Hourly the choices are 4, 8, 12 and 24 hours (8 by default), which the Private Cloud Controller calculates from the time backups are enabled; for Daily a time in 15-minute intervals (12 AM by default); for Weekly a day and time (Monday at 12 AM by default). The schedule is based on the time zone of the Private Cloud Controller. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:backup-schedule] The wait before App Connectors and Private Service Edges switch to Business Continuity is an integer of minutes or hours; by default 2 minutes is selected, the minimum is 1 minute and the maximum is 2 hours, or 120 minutes. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:max-wait-to-enter] Users are switched back to the public cloud either Automatic (the default), when there are no active connections, or Time-Based, after the maximum wait time to exit Business Continuity. That time is how long Private Service Edges wait before sending Client Connector a message to try to re-establish the control and data channel to the Zero Trust Exchange; by default 24 hours is selected, the minimum is 10 minutes and the maximum is 7 days, 168 hours or 10,080 minutes. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:switch-users-to-public-cloud] Business Continuity authenticates users with either the existing IdP, the same configuration used for user authentication in normal operations, or a new IdP configured by uploading its metadata file; the service provider URL, certificate and entity ID to give a new IdP are based on the entered Business Continuity Domain. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:idp-configuration]

**Entering.** When connectivity to the Zero Trust Exchange is lost, a timer starts based on the Max Time to Wait to Enter Business Continuity setting. App Connectors and Private Service Edges retry connections to the ZTE until it expires, then connect to a Private Cloud Controller using the Business Continuity FQDNs, and after connecting continue to reestablish the control channel to the Private Access cloud. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:enter-components] For a new user enrolling in Business Continuity after launching Client Connector, Client Connector first attempts to reach the cloud authentication endpoint (samlsp.private.zscaler.com); if it is not reachable, Client Connector contacts the local authentication endpoint and, after successful authentication, enters Business Continuity. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:enter-new-users] For an enrolled user, if the cloud authentication endpoint is not reachable Client Connector attempts the local authentication endpoint hosted on the Private Cloud Controller, and if that is reachable the user is redirected to the IdP and authenticates with the Business Continuity IdP. Client Connector then contacts the ZTE for a list of Public Service Edges or Private Service Edges; if the initial connection to the ZTE fails or no control and data channel can be established with any of them, it contacts the Business Continuity FQDN and the user is redirected to the Business Continuity Private Service Edges. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:enter-enrolled-users] If the connection of an authenticated user is idle for 60 seconds, Client Connector attempts to reconnect to the current Public Service Edge that the ZTE redirected the user to. If it cannot reach any of the Public Service Edges or Private Service Edge after two retries, it contacts the Private Cloud Controller using the Business Continuity FQDN, and the Private Cloud Controller redirects users to the Business Continuity Private Service Edges. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:enter-connected-users]

**Exiting.** App Connectors and Private Service Edges exit Business Continuity and switch their control channels to the Private Access cloud immediately after connectivity to the Private Access cloud is restored. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:exit-components] The Private Service Edge sends a Remote Procedure Call to move users back to the ZTE. After the initial connection it waits for 10 minutes and probes every minute for active M-Tunnels, and sends the RPC when there are no active M-Tunnels for a given user or when the Max Time to Exit Business Continuity Mode expires; the timers are unique to each user connection. If the ZTE is reachable Client Connector connects to it, otherwise it stays connected to the Private Service Edges and waits for the next RPC. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:exit-users]

**Limitations.** For new users who enrolled during active Business Continuity, mutual TLS authentication between users and Private Service Edges is not supported, and SCIM attributes are not supported: only SAML attributes are supported for access policies, timeout policies and client forwarding policies. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:limits-new-users] While in active Business Continuity, configuration changes, including deployment of Private Service Edges and App Connectors, are not supported. The Browser Access, Source IP Anchoring, Cloud Connector and Branch Connector client types are not supported. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:limits-no-config-changes] Certificates for App Connectors, Private Service Edges and Private Cloud Controllers cannot be renewed while in active Business Continuity. These components maintain certificate validity for one year and can be renewed earlier by specifying the Certificate Renewal Threshold when configuring a Private Cloud. They do not automatically upgrade to Business Continuity. [zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:limits-certificates-upgrades] Whether client platforms other than Windows and macOS are supported is open [gap:zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:bc-platform-support]. What several of the settings mean in practice is open [gap:zpa.private-service-edges.business-continuity:bc-semantics].

Client Connector's side is in [Business Continuity in Client Connector: availability, the Business Continuity page, the app profile switch, install parameters and enrolling during an outage](kb:zcc.install.business-continuity). Disaster recovery is a separate mode: [Disaster recovery mode and Private Service Edge groups](kb:zpa.private-service-edges.disaster-recovery).

What to check:
1. Whether every application and access policy was configured before Business Continuity started, since configuration changes are not supported while it is active.
2. Whether the name a component or client fails to resolve is one of the Business Continuity FQDNs, and whether it resolves from DNS servers the organization hosts.
3. Whether a user who enrolled during active Business Continuity relies on SCIM attributes or mutual TLS, which are not supported for them.
4. Whether on-premises components were due a certificate renewal, which cannot happen while Business Continuity is active.
