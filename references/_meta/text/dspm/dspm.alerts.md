# How DSPM alerts work: risk level, statuses, snooze and resolve, alert details, and the alert rules and ignore alert filters that act on them (dspm.alerts)
Also called: dspm alert status; dspm snooze alert; dspm resolve alert; dspm alert notifications; dspm alert rules; dspm ignore alert filter; dspm policy exceptions; dspm alert graph

An alert is the outcome of a policy evaluation on a resource type; in the page's example, a policy enabled to detect publicly exposed S3 buckets containing sensitive data produces 5 alerts when 5 such buckets exist. [dspm.alerts:alert-definition] An alert's risk level (Critical, High, Medium or Low) is the average of the policy severity and the resource impact; the page's example gives High for a Critical policy and Medium resource impact. [dspm.alerts:alert-risk-level] On the Grouped by Resource tab, a resource's risk level depends on the highest risk level among the alerts generated for it. [dspm.alerts:resource-risk-level] Alert Age is the number of days an alert was in the open state, counted from zero when the alert is generated or reset. [dspm.alerts:alert-age]

**Alert tabs.** The All Alerts tab shows alerts with an open status by default, and an alert's status is one of Open, Closed, Resolved or Snoozed. [dspm.alerts:all-alerts-defaults] The Grouped by Policy tab shows policies with an open alert status by default. [dspm.alerts:grouped-policy-default] On the Grouped by Resource tab, resources with an open alert status are shown by default, and the Grouped by Resource page says the Vulnerabilities tab of a resource is displayed only for EC2 instances. [dspm.alerts:grouped-resource-default-vuln] The Alert Details tab shows the policy's MITRE ATT&CK technique and ID and the compliance benchmark associated with the policy. [dspm.alerts:alert-detail-fields] An alert's Sensitive Data tab is visible only for alerts from policies that detect sensitive data, and its Malware tab only for alerts from policies that detect malware. [dspm.alerts:alert-detail-tabs] The Remediation tab gives steps to perform in the CSP; once the violation is addressed there, all alerts generated for that policy are resolved and move from Open to Resolved. [dspm.alerts:remediation]

**Graph.** The alert graph is interactive only for open and snoozed alerts; for resolved and closed alerts it is frozen, and it is reactivated for a resolved alert only when the alert is reset and moves to open. [dspm.alerts:graph-interactivity] The alert graph is a subset of the Resource Inventory graph; its sensitive data path shows only associated resources that contain sensitive data with the matched DLP engines. [dspm.alerts:graph-vs-inventory]

**Actions.** Unsnooze is available only when an alert is snoozed, and Reset only when it is resolved. [dspm.alerts:actions-availability] The Managing Alerts page says resolving an alert requires an administrator role or a custom role with the Resolve Alerts permission, which also grants permission to reset manually resolved alerts. [dspm.alerts:resolve-permission] A manual resolve applies only in the Zscaler Admin Console, the policy violation persists in the cloud service provider, and the alert stays resolved until reset. [dspm.alerts:resolve-local-only] Snoozing an alert pauses its notifications to recipients and third-party integrations for the snooze period, which defaults to 1 day; the Managing Alerts page says snoozing needs an administrator role or a custom role with the Snooze Alerts permission, which also grants permission to reset snoozed alerts. [dspm.alerts:snooze-behaviour] Whether every administrator role can resolve or snooze is in dispute [divergence:dspm.alerts:resolve-role-scope]. Unsnoozing or resetting an alert updates its status from the latest policy findings. [dspm.alerts:unsnooze-reset-recompute]

**Status lifecycle.** Newly generated alerts are Open and later move to Resolved, Closed or Snoozed. [dspm.alerts:status-initial] An open alert moves to resolved, closed or snoozed from the latest policy findings, manual actions, or an ignore alert filter; the page's Open row says that when a data store is removed from the scan settings its alerts remain open based on the data store's posture assessment, while its Closed row lists a data store removed earlier and not added back to the scan settings as a cause of closing. [dspm.alerts:status-open] An automatically resolved alert moves back to Open if the issue is detected within 180 days of resolving it, and DSPM generates a new alert if the violation is detected again after 180 days. [dspm.alerts:status-reopen-180] Closed is the final state: the page lists as causes an offboarded or deleted cloud account, an edited, disabled or deleted policy, a deleted data store, and a data store removed earlier that is not added back to the scan settings, and a closed alert is deleted from the Zscaler Admin Console after 180 days. [dspm.alerts:status-closed] A manually resolved alert stays resolved until reset, and a resolved alert moves to Closed if its data store, policy or cloud account is deleted. [dspm.alerts:status-resolved] The Alert Status page says a snoozed alert stays snoozed even if the violation is fixed in the CSP; on unsnooze or expiry it becomes open or resolved from the latest findings; if its data store or policy is deleted it becomes closed regardless of the snooze period. [dspm.alerts:status-snoozed]

Whether a deleted resource or policy closes a snoozed alert is in dispute [divergence:dspm.alerts:snoozed-delete-closes]. Whether an edited or disabled policy closes its alerts is in dispute [divergence:dspm.alerts:policy-change-closes].

**Alert rules.** Alert rules export alert notifications to third-party integrations whenever an alert's status changes, and DSPM must be integrated with those tools first. [dspm.alerts:rule-purpose] What the third-party integrations support is open [gap:dspm.alerts:third-party-integrations]. An alert rule's Notification Scope needs at least one filter, and the Policies, Cloud Accounts, Tags, Resources and DLP Engines filters default to All Policies, All Account, All Tags, All Resources and All DLP Engines respectively. [dspm.alerts:rule-scope-defaults] For alert rules, the policy filter can select by threat category (Public Exposure, External Users, Risky Credentials, Malicious Access, Data Loss, Data Governance) or severity (Critical, High, Medium, Low), and the cloud provider filter offers AWS, Azure or GCP. [dspm.alerts:rule-scope-values] The ITSM/Ticketing channel of an alert rule takes ServiceNow, JIRA or both, an assignee email, and optional notifications for closed and resolved alerts; ticking Remediation includes remediation steps in the alert payload. [dspm.alerts:rule-channel-itsm] An alert rule can also send to a Cloud Storage service through an integration account (with an optional Remediation payload checkbox) or to ChatOps, where Slack and its channels are selected. [dspm.alerts:rule-channel-storage-chatops]

**Alert rule state.** If no third-party tool is selected when adding an alert rule, the rule is disabled and shown with an exclamation mark as invalid; tools can be added later by editing, and a rule is shown enabled by default after the wizard. [dspm.alerts:rule-no-tools-disabled] An alert rule is disabled with an exclamation mark when its third-party tools were not configured or its selected filters were deleted, and it can be enabled only when at least one filter scope is available and at least one third-party tool is configured. [dspm.alerts:rule-invalid] Disabling an alert rule stops DSPM sending alert notifications to the configured third-party tools. [dspm.alerts:rule-disable-effect]

**Ignore alert filters.** Ignore alert filters are managed on the Ignore Alert Filter page, reached under Data Security > DSPM > Policy Exceptions, and are enabled by default. [dspm.alerts:ignore-location] Adding an ignore alert filter stops DSPM generating alerts for the selected filter scope and stops notifications to third-party integrations. [dspm.alerts:ignore-effect] A new ignore alert filter is enabled by default and newly triggered alerts matching its criteria are ignored. [dspm.alerts:ignore-added-default] An ignore alert filter needs at least one filter in its scope, defaults the policy filter to All Policies, and its cloud provider filter offers AWS, Azure, GCP, On-Premises or Snowflake. [dspm.alerts:ignore-scope] An ignore alert filter is disabled and marked invalid when its selected scope is deleted, except that deleting only one of several selected policies, accounts or resource types does not affect it; it can be enabled only once at least one filter scope is available. [dspm.alerts:ignore-invalid] Editing or deleting ignore alert filters affects only newly triggered alerts. [dspm.alerts:ignore-new-only]

Which inputs the risk level averages is open [gap:dspm.alerts:risk-inputs].

What to check:
1. Whether the alert is Open, Resolved, Closed or Snoozed, and which of the page's listed causes moved it.
2. Whether a snoozed alert whose resource or policy was deleted stays snoozed or is closed: the DSPM pages disagree.
3. Whether a disabled or edited policy closes its alerts: the DSPM pages disagree.
4. The roles that can resolve or snooze an alert: the Resolve Alerts or Snooze Alerts permission; the pages disagree on whether any administrator role, View-Only Administrator included, qualifies.
5. Whether the alert rule or ignore alert filter is shown disabled with an exclamation mark.

Sources disagree (name both sides):
- The Managing Alerts page says a snoozed alert stays snoozed if its resource or policy is deleted; the Alert Status page says it moves to closed. [divergence:dspm.alerts:snoozed-delete-closes]
  - zscaler-help: A snoozed alert remains snoozed for the specified duration if the resource or policy is deleted.
  - zscaler-help: If the data store or policy is deleted while the alert is snoozed, the alert moves to closed irrespective of the snooze period.
  Impact: The status of a snoozed alert after a policy or data store is deleted is read differently on the two pages; check the console rather than assume either.
- The Managing Alerts page says an administrator role or a custom role can resolve or snooze alerts; the permissions page gives Resolve Alerts and Snooze Alerts to some predefined roles but not View-Only Administrator, which the About Access Roles page calls a default administrator role. [divergence:dspm.alerts:resolve-role-scope]
  - zscaler-help: Resolving an alert requires an administrator role or a custom role with the Resolve Alerts permission.
  - zscaler-help: View-Only Administrator is a default administrator role.
  - zscaler-help: The View-Only Administrator role's alerts module carries only Export Alerts and View Alerts, with no Resolve Alerts or Snooze Alerts.
  Impact: Whether every administrator role, View-Only Administrator included, can resolve or snooze alerts is read differently on the pages; check the admin's role against the permissions list in [[dspm.roles]].
- The Alert Status page lists a policy being edited or disabled as a cause of closed alerts; the Managing Policies page says disabling leaves existing alerts unchanged and only a query-logic edit closes them. [divergence:dspm.alerts:policy-change-closes]
  - zscaler-help: A policy being edited, disabled or deleted is a cause of closed alerts.
  - zscaler-help: Disabling a policy leaves existing alerts unchanged, and editing a custom policy closes alerts only when the query logic is edited.
  Impact: Whether alerts close when a policy is disabled, or edited without changing its query logic, is read differently on the two pages.
